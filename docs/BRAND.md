# mogkit — Brand Message

The verbal brand: the name, what it means, how mogkit sounds, and what it says. This is a context document — site copy, README, and wedge pages inherit this voice.

**Scope note:** This covers the *verbal* brand only. Visual identity — color, logo, typography — is intentionally out of scope and will be defined separately.

---

## The name

**mogkit** — *mog* + *kit*.

- **mog** — to out-craft the room. To be, visibly and undeniably, the sharpest operator at the table.
- **kit** — the bundle you install to get there.

mogkit is the kit that makes you mog. The wordmark is always lowercase, one word: `mogkit`. It is a CLI tool; it looks like one.

---

## mog, defined

This definition is the spine of the brand. It reframes the slang as *craft*, not dominance — and that reframe is non-negotiable. Use it on the landing page.

> **mog** /mɒɡ/ — *verb*
> To outclass through visible craft. Not louder. Not flashier. Unmistakably better at the work.
> *"She mogged the roadmap review — every assumption sourced, every gap named."*

mog is about substance. The moment the brand lets "mog" drift toward hollow superiority or status, the name's worst connotation wins. Always anchor it to the work.

## Brand thesis

Most PM tools make your work *look* better. mogkit makes it *be* better.

It will not write your PRD. It will interrogate it — what your evidence supports, what you're assuming, who you haven't talked to — until what you write is bulletproof. It runs in your terminal. It's open source. It's built on the methods the best product leaders already use.

You don't get a shortcut. You get sharper.

---

## Messaging pillars

Four pillars. Every piece of copy should ladder up to one of them.

**1. Craft, not output.**
mogkit never hands you a finished deliverable. It surfaces what your evidence supports, what you're assuming without proof, and what you haven't asked. You write the doc — and it's a better doc. We sell sharper judgment, not faster typing.

**2. Local and yours.**
Runs in your terminal. Version-controlled. Open source, MIT. No SaaS, no login, no lock-in. Your research becomes an asset you own and grow, not data in someone else's cloud.

**3. The on-ramp.**
mogkit takes you from a PM who lives in Notion to a PM who works like an engineer. Every step is small. Every step earns the next. We don't gatekeep the terminal — we make it approachable.

**4. By the craft, for the craft.**
Methodology-backed — continuous discovery, Working Backwards, the real traditions. Community-built skills. The library compounds as PMs contribute to it.

## Voice & tone

**Confident, not arrogant.** We believe in the kit. We never talk down to the user. The user is the hero; mogkit is the kit they pick up.

**Dry, not zany.** Funny the way a sharp senior PM is funny — understatement, not exclamation marks. The name is already a wink. The copy doesn't need to elbow you in the ribs.

**Plain-spoken.** A PM tool that buries you in PM jargon has failed. Short words. Real sentences. Say the thing.

**Honest about limits.** We say plainly what mogkit doesn't do. Honesty is the brand — it mirrors the product's own provenance principle. Overclaiming is off-brand.

**Lean into the bit — in the right places.** "mog" is funny and we use it that way. The looksmaxxing register is fair game *as obvious satire* — the audience knows the joke. But concentrate the humor where it's cheap to edit later: README intro, CLI flavor text, empty states, the footer, social copy. Keep it *out* of the positioning statement and pillar headlines — those stay straight, because satire dates faster than craft and the substance has to land for someone who skips every joke. Funny in the voice; rigorous in the bones. Never explain the joke — if you know, you know.

### Voice in practice

| Off-brand | On-brand |
|---|---|
| "Supercharge your PM workflow with AI-powered synergy!" | "Install the kit. Out-think the room." |
| "mogkit dominates the competition." | "mogkit makes you the sharpest operator in the room." |
| "Leverage cutting-edge tooling to optimize your discovery process." | "Point it at your interview notes. It shows you what you actually know — and what you're guessing." |
| "Generate a complete PRD in seconds!" | "It won't write your PRD. It'll make you write a better one." |

## Using "mog" for laughs

The bit is a feature. Here are sanctioned places to land it — all low-stakes, easy to refresh later. The positioning statement and pillar headlines are *not* on this list; they stay straight.

**README intro line**
> The PM mogged everyone in the room. He had `mogkit`.

**CLI — first run / `mogkit init` success**
> `✓ workspace ready. you are now legally allowed to mog.`

**CLI — `mogkit doctor` all-clear**
> `✓ everything checks out. you are mogging within acceptable parameters.`

**CLI — empty corpus state**
> `no sources yet. can't mog on vibes — run "mogkit add".`

**`mogkit status` on a thin corpus**
> `corpus: thin. respectfully, this is not yet a mogging-grade evidence base.`

**Site footer**
> `mogkit — discovery-maxxing for product managers.`

**Wedge page subhead (Discovery)**
> Stop guessing in roadmap reviews. Start mogging with provenance.

**Social / launch post**
> Most PM tools help you look productive. mogkit helps you actually mog. Open source, runs in your terminal.

**404 page**
> This page got mogged out of existence. Head back to the docs.

Rules for the bit: punch at the *work*, never at the user — the user is always the one doing the mogging, never the one getting mogged. Keep each joke to one line. If a joke needs a setup, it's too long. And the satire only works because the tool underneath is genuinely rigorous — the moment the product is mediocre, the jokes read as cope.

## What we say / what we never say

**Say:** out-craft · sharpen · level up · sharpest operator in the room · craft · interrogate · out-think · the kit · the path. The "-maxxing" / mogging register is fair game in the humor surfaces (see *Using "mog" for laughs*) — as satire, kept light, punching at the work.

**Never say:** anything that frames mog as hollow status, or that punches *at the user* — "you got mogged," "beat your coworkers," "crush the competition," "looks" as a real measure of worth. The user is always the one mogging, never the target. In the straight copy — positioning, pillars — mog means craft and substance, full stop.

## Naming & usage conventions

- **Wordmark:** lowercase `mogkit`, one word. Never *MogKit*, *Mog Kit*, or *MOGKIT* in running text. (A logo lockup may style it differently — that is the visual identity's call.)
- **CLI command:** `mogkit` — e.g. `mogkit init`, `mogkit add`, `mogkit status`. Subcommands stay plain and professional; the wrapper carries the personality, the internals do not.
- **npm package:** `mogkit`. Verify it is available before launch — a free domain with a taken package handle is a dead end.
- **Repo home:** published on the maintainer's personal GitHub account — `github.com/<maintainer>/mogkit` — not a separate org. The repo README does the brand work; the account namespace does not need to.
- **In a sentence:** "mogkit" starts lowercase even at the start of a sentence is acceptable for the wordmark, but if that reads awkwardly, rephrase so it isn't sentence-initial rather than capitalizing it.
